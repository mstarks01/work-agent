## Question

Design the eval suite: which golden cases (OWASP Threat Model Cookbook conversions + which internal systems), the reference threat-model format, scoring method (LLM-judge semantic matching for coverage/recall + precision + severity calibration, plus structural checks), pass thresholds, and CI wiring. Includes the one-time SME blessing workflow for the cases themselves.

## Resolution

Resolved 2026-07-20 via grilling, grounded in tickets 001 (published threat-modeling eval baselines), 004 (topology), 005 (report schema and severity matrix), 007 (pinned models), 013/019/020 (prompts and exemplars), and the shipped `stride_service` package.

1. **A golden case is a two-artifact fixture, not an input/output pair.** Each case carries a blessed `SystemModel` *and* a reference threat set written against that model's element IDs. The pipeline has two LLM stages that fail differently — `extract` (text → model) and analysts/critic (model → threats) — and an end-to-end-only fixture cannot attribute a regression to either: a recall miss could equally be an analyst failure or an element `extract` never produced. Two artifacts buy three eval modes from one corpus: **extraction** (text vs blessed model), **analysis** (blessed model injected at `prepare`, vs reference threats — deterministic input, so threat numbers are attributable to analysts/critic alone), and **end-to-end** (integration smoke). It is also what makes reference threats *writable at all*: threats cite `affected_element_ids`, which only exist against a pinned model.

2. **Blessed models are bootstrapped from `extract`, then SME-corrected.** Hand-authoring a DFD per case is the expensive path for no gain the correction pass doesn't also give. *Risk recorded, not solved:* bootstrapping anchors the reviewer — correcting a plausible artifact is measurably less thorough than blank-page authoring, so `extract`'s systematic blind spots can survive into its own ground truth and extraction-eval scores read optimistically. Mitigations: the SME works a checklist against the **source text**, not against the candidate model, and the bootstrap→blessed diff is kept per case as signal about what `extract` habitually gets wrong.

3. **`ReferenceThreat` is a dedicated eval-side model, deliberately not `DraftThreat`.** Fields: `category`, `affected_element_ids`, `claim` (one sentence, attacker-action phrasing — the semantic matching target), `tier` (`must-find` | `expected`), `severity` (`likelihood`/`impact` only, band derived as in prod), `notes` (SME rationale, never scored). Reusing `DraftThreat` was rejected twice over: it carries fields that must **not** be graded (`mitigations`, a 4000-char `description` — grading prose the model was never asked to reproduce verbatim) and lacks the field that makes thresholds meaningful. `id` is actively misleading — reference `S-01` and produced `S-01` are the same string for no reason. Vocabularies (`StrideCategory`, `Rating`, severity derivation) are imported from `report.py` so they cannot drift; a lint asserts every `affected_element_ids` entry resolves in the blessed model, mirroring ticket 020's exemplar guard.

4. **Two tiers, because one weight makes every threshold wrong.** `must-find` means *missing it means the tool does not work* and drives the hard recall gate; `expected` is legitimate-but-skippable and drives a tracked, softer number. A single tier forces the bar either too low to catch real breakage or permanently unreachable.

5. **Cases are small on purpose: 8–20 elements.** Ground truth must be *exhaustively* enumerable by a human, because non-exhaustive references silently corrupt precision — every real threat the SME didn't think to write down would score as a false positive. Small systems are the only ones where "not in the reference" carries information.

6. **Corpus composition is driven by the domain-bias measurement.** Full corpus is 12: 4 OWASP Threat Model Cookbook conversions (public, redistributable, community-vetted DFDs halve authoring cost), 6 internal systems, 2 adversarial/degenerate (a sparse input that should yield heavy `unknown` + `needs-info`, and one that should fail the validity gate and exercise `repair` → `reject`). **Exactly one case must be a payment/checkout system** — near-identical in shape to `analyst.md`'s shared exemplar — and at least three structurally far from it (IoT fleet, batch data pipeline, ML inference service). The map's exemplar-domain-bias risk is not measurable as an absolute score, only as the **delta** between near- and far-exemplar recall; without the payments control there is nothing to subtract from and that fog line stays fog permanently.

7. **Matching is mechanical where it can be, judged only where it can't.** (a) **Mechanical lane prefilter** — a produced threat is only ever a candidate for same-category references. Not a shortcut: ticket 013 already ruled that misfiled threats are rejected, never recategorized. Cuts pair count ~6× for free. (b) **Per-pair LLM judge on `claim` equivalence within lane** — binary match plus a one-line rationale, over small sets (~15 reference × ~10 produced per case), parallelizable and individually auditable in the run artifact. The judge rules on exactly one thing: same attacker action against the same target. (c) **Mechanical one-to-one assignment** — deterministic bipartite matching so each produced threat consumes at most one reference; without it recall is inflatable and stops meaning anything. Single-call whole-set judging was rejected for losing auditability and the one-to-one guarantee.

8. **Element agreement is scored, not filtered.** Requiring a shared `affected_element_ids` entry as a second prefilter was considered and rejected: a correct threat may cite the data flow where the SME cited the process at its endpoint, scoring a hit as a miss. Matching is decided on the claim; element agreement is its own reported dimension, so "matched, but on the wrong element" is a visible outcome rather than a silent pass or silent fail. This yields three independent metrics instead of one mushy score — **recall**, **lane accuracy** (categorization: a documented failure mode from arXiv:2505.04101, otherwise invisible), **element accuracy** (traceability, the property the whole report schema exists for). Severity calibration is fully mechanical: for matched pairs, compare produced `likelihood`/`impact` to the reference's and report the confusion — `derive_severity_level` is shipped arithmetic, no judge involved.

9. **Unmatched threats are adjudicated into three buckets; only one gates.** "Unmatched = false positive" was rejected as actively harmful: references are non-exhaustive by construction, so that rule punishes finding real threats and every tuning cycle would push toward under-reporting — self-defeating when baseline recall is the binding constraint (<0.30 off-the-shelf, ticket 001 §2). Each unmatched threat is judged against the blessed model into: **`ungrounded`** (asserts a fact the model does not support — nonexistent element, control claimed absent when the attribute is `unknown`, an underived crossing; **the only gating bucket**, because hallucination is what destroys trust in a security report), **`valid-unlisted`** (plausible and grounded, simply not in the reference — *not a failure*, tracked), **`noise`** (grounded but vacuous or near-duplicate — tracked, soft). Partly mechanical already: `critic.py` enforces reference resolution, so a class of ungroundedness fails before the judge runs. **`needs-info` is never a false positive** — it is the designed behavior the third exemplar per category teaches, and scores in its own bucket.

10. **Critic yield: score both sides of the critic.** The same run scores the pre-critic merged union *and* the post-critic confirmed set, giving how many ungrounded threats the critic killed and how many real ones it killed with them. This is the direct empirical test of ticket 004's generator–critic bet against its published comparators (Semgrep ~20% kill at 92–96% reviewer agreement; ~86% raw FP on unfiltered LLM security findings). Without it the most expensive node in the graph is unfalsifiable.

11. **Corpus feedback loop.** Recurring `valid-unlisted` threats are surfaced in the run artifact for SME review and promoted into the reference set at the next blessing pass. Non-exhaustive ground truth converges from real output; it never converges from someone trying to be exhaustive up front.

12. **Judge: `pro` tier, Google-only, separately pinned.** Self-preference bias is tolerable here specifically because decision 7 scoped the judge to claim *equivalence* — a near-symmetric relation, not a quality preference, which is where self-enhancement bias actually bites. A cross-vendor judge adds a second provider to a Google-only stack for marginal gain; rejected. The judge model string is **versioned separately from the tiers**, because a judge upgrade silently re-scores history and turns a comparison into a phantom regression — changing it is an explicit re-baselining event, not a dependency bump. Pair order presented to the judge is randomized to kill position bias.

13. **Calibrate the judge against humans once, with a stated bar.** In the same session that blesses the corpus, the SME hand-labels ~100 candidate pairs match/no-match; judge–human agreement must be **≥90%** (comparator: Semgrep's 92–96% on an analogous triage task). Failing the bar means the judge prompt needs work, not ship-anyway. Re-run on any judge model change — that is what makes re-baselining a real gate. Those labeled pairs persist as fixtures and are how the scorer gets unit-tested offline with zero Vertex calls. **Consequence to state plainly: recall/precision here are judge-relative.** Valid for tracking movement and comparing configurations; *not* absolutes comparable to ThreatModeling-LLM's 0.73, and nobody should later quote them as such.

14. **The judge prompt does not live in `prompts/`.** That tree is the shipped service's, governed by ticket 020's lints (four exact H2s, token caps, `DraftThreat`-parsing exemplars) and baked into the production container. A judge prompt satisfies none of those rules and has no business in the deployed image; it lives in the eval tree under its own conventions.

15. **Sampling config is pinned in versioned config, shared by eval and production, defaulting to `0`.** Grading a configuration you don't ship is the failure mode where the suite goes green while production drifts, so the eval-only-temperature-0 split was rejected. Pinned model strings (ticket 007) exist for reproducibility and library-default sampling gives most of it back. **But the value stays a knob, not a hardcoded constant**, because temperature 0 has a specific cost here: greedy decoding most closely reproduces the anchored exemplar pattern, plausibly *amplifying* the very domain bias of decision 6, and it forecloses ticket 001 §4.4's cheapest recall lever (Self-MoA, k=2–3 at temperature > 0, union-merged and critic-deduped). The number is therefore **the first thing the suite measures, not an input to it**: once the corpus lands, run 0 vs. production default vs. k=3 union and let the far-domain cases decide. This fog line is not resolvable by argument, and the instrument that resolves it is what this ticket builds.

16. **Thresholds: decide the mechanism now, derive the numbers from measurement.** Any number picked today would be fabricated, and a fabricated gate either flaps or gets ignored. Three tiers by nature: **Tier 1 structural** — report parses as `StrideReport`, refs resolve, IDs unique with correct category letters, severity bands match `derive_severity_level`, summary counts consistent: absolute, per-case, zero tolerance, **gating from day one** (deterministic, already enforced by shipped validators, zero flake, free). **Tier 2 must-find recall** — absolute and **per-case**, since aggregate hides a single case failing completely, which is the failure that matters; target 100%, phased in. **Tier 3 everything else** (expected-tier recall, ungrounded rate, lane accuracy, element accuracy, critic yield, severity calibration) — **regression ratchets against checked-in baselines, never absolutes**, which is the only robust form for a judge-relative, sampling-dependent metric: you detect *movement*, and moving a baseline is a reviewable diff with a human explaining why. Phase-in: land with Tier 1 gating and Tiers 2–3 observe-only, run the full sweep ~5× to establish baseline and variance (the same exercise that answers decision 15), then promote Tier 2 to a hard gate and derive Tier 3's noise bands from the observed spread. A gate that fires before anyone knows the normal range trains people to bypass it. **One tracked, deliberately non-gating number:** the near- vs far-exemplar recall delta — a large delta is a finding to act on, not a build to break.

17. **CI: the offline/live split is the load-bearing structure.** **Offline evals** (schema lints, reference resolution, severity arithmetic, judge-scorer unit tests over the recorded label fixtures) need no credentials and run on every PR. **Live evals** call real Vertex and run on a credentialed trigger. Vertex authenticates via **IAM, not API keys** — API keys are the AI Studio surface and cannot serve ticket 007's pinned regional model strings, so there is no key-based option that doesn't abandon Vertex. Auth is **GitHub OIDC → Workload Identity Federation → short-lived credentials, zero long-lived secrets**, via a **separate eval service account** from the deploy identity holding only `roles/aiplatform.user`, with the pool's attribute condition pinned to this repository. Because nothing in `src/` reads `GOOGLE_*` or constructs a client — ADK relies on ADC implicitly — the eval suite needs **no code change to authenticate**. Confirmed with the user: the repo will be on GitHub with **no fork contributions**, so per-PR live evals are technically viable. **`pull_request_target` is refused regardless**: combined with a PR-head checkout it executes untrusted code holding the Vertex identity — a credential-exfiltration primitive, not a CI convenience. Live evals are **path-filtered** to `prompts/**`, `skills/**`, `config/**`, and `src/stride_service/{graph,prompts,skills,critic,pipeline,report,system_model}.py` — `report.py`/`system_model.py` count as agentic because they define the schemas bound as node output schemas — plus a **scheduled full sweep** as the backstop, since a path filter can always be fooled.

18. **Cost is not the constraint; the earlier framing was wrong.** Per case: 1 flash (`extract`) + 6 pro (analysts, concurrent behind the `JoinNode`) + 1 pro (`critic`) + judge — 8 calls but only ~3 sequential waits. Roughly 69K pro input / 21K pro output per case using the token figures the closed tickets established (analyst composed instruction 4.7–4.9K, critic skill 2.3K, 8–20 element model ~3K), i.e. **~$0.30/case and ~$3.60 for a 12-case sweep** at Gemini 2.5 Pro's published rate — *order-of-magnitude, to be confirmed against current Vertex pricing before anyone quotes it*. The real constraints are wall-clock (~2–4 min/case, independent and parallelizable), shared quota, and nondeterminism (decision 15) — not dollars.

19. **Phase 1 is deliberately smaller than the design.** The expensive line item is **SME time**, serialized on a human, not code (the spine is roughly a scorer module, a fixture loader, and a workflow file). Phase 1 ships **6 cases** — the payments control, three far-domain systems, two cookbook conversions — chosen to preserve the one measurement that cannot be reconstructed later (decision 6); the degenerate/repair cases and the remaining six defer. Phase 1 gates on **Tier 1 structural only**; must-find recall reports without blocking. Deferred to phase 2 with their design already fixed above: critic yield (needs extra instrumentation to expose pre-critic drafts), ratchets/baselines/noise bands (meaningless before baseline runs exist), and the element-accuracy / `noise` / `valid-unlisted` / domain-delta reporting refinements over the same matched pairs.

Graduated tickets 022 (author the phase-1 corpus + blessing workflow), 023 (implement the eval harness, `ReferenceThreat`, scorer, and pinned sampling config), 024 (GitHub Actions CI wiring with WIF and the offline/live split), and 025 (phase 2: baselines, ratchets, critic yield, corpus expansion), wired 022 → 023 → 024 → 025 — content before code, per the same order tickets 019/020 used.
