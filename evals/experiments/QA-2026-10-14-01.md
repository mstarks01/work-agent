# QA-2026-10-14-01: a separate reader that states capabilities from a description

Only an answer or a closed field states a capability (ADRs 0051 and 0085), so
a description that says "Customers sign in through Auth0" still meets the
question "Does the application take part in OAuth?". This audit measured a
separate model reader: it reads one description and the capability table,
and states each capability the text says the application has, with the words
that say it. It states no absence, by the maintainer's choice. Code keeps a
statement only where the key is in the table and the quote is in the
description under the gate's matcher. The application is not touched; the
harness is `QA-2026-10-14-01/capability_reader.py`; round 1 ran the prompt
as it stands at commit e97ab927, and round 2 the prompt with rules 6 and 7.
After the runs, the harness's vendor lookup changed from a named vendor to
`vendor_for_route`, which resolves the same vendor from the model route.

`gpt-5.6-luna` on OpenAI, 19 calls: one pre-flight and four more runs on the
example, and one run on each of the 14 development cases (the two holdouts
are not read). About $0.07 to $0.08 of a $0.30 grant: 82,914 prompt and
49,840 completion tokens. The runs are `preflight.json`, `example-runs.json`
and `case-runs.json`.

## Round 1: what the reader states (E1)

Four runs on the example kept 36 to 40 statements; one kept 3 and stopped
early. Of 375 statements over all runs, code refused 10: 9 quotes were not
in the description, and 1 quote was empty. No key was outside the table.

On the example's checkpoint model with lever A's statements in, a full run
of the reader takes ASVS level 2 from 30 capability questions listed to 23,
the questions off the list from 67 to 54-56, and the units that apply with no
answer from 59 to 110-112. Eight questions the owner answers "yes" leave the
list. The run that stopped early takes the list to 28 and the units to 75.
At level 1 the list falls from 8 to 5 and the units rise from 13 to 31.

## Round 1: precision (E1)

The maintainer read every distinct statement, 254 rows (`ballot-issued.csv`,
returned as `ballot-returned.csv`; every carried column unchanged):

| Scope | Right | Wrong | Unsure | Precision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example (44 rows) | 43 | 1 | 0 | 97.7% |
| 14 cases (210 rows) | 196 | 11 | 3 | 94.7% |
| All | 239 | 12 | 3 | 95.2% |

Two places hold 10 of the 12 wrong rows:

- `database`, 5 wrong of 11, all `too-specific`: the description names a
  database but no query language or technology, and the meaning reads "with a
  query language".
- Case 12, the over-claiming supplier portal, 5 wrong, all `third-party`: the
  reader gave the application what the vendor's portal does (`authentication`,
  `password-authentication`, `encryption`, `multi-step-flow`,
  `spreadsheet-export`), though the prompt's rule 4 forbids it.

The other two are `native-client` on the example (a browser app) and
`mail-sending` on case 09 (a fax gateway). The three `unsure` rows are
`cryptographic-keys` on case 02, `sensitive-data` on case 12 and `sessions`
on case 13.

Fifteen right rows carry a note that the quote alone does not show the
capability and the description shows it elsewhere (rows 8, 9, 10, 30, 40, 43,
57, 88, 90, 156, 170, 205, 214, 244, 245). The gate checks that a quote is in
the description, not that it shows the capability, so such a statement is
right but cites weak evidence.

A wrong "present" makes a requirement apply that does not; it never rules
one out. The reader's prompt is unmeasured on the holdout cases.

## Round 2: two rules for the two error sources (E2)

Rule 6 says that a vendor's hosted product the team uses is not part of the
application, with an example outside the corpus (a hosted helpdesk). Rule 7
says that `database` needs a named query language or database technology.
The same 19 calls ran again (`example-runs-v2.json`, `case-runs-v2.json`),
about $0.07, so the audit spent about $0.15 of the $0.30.

None of the 12 statements ruled wrong came back. The example's five runs kept
36 to 38 statements each, and none stopped early. Of 238 distinct statements,
213 were statements the first ballot holds and keep its verdict. The other 25
went to a second ballot (`ballot-2-issued.csv`, returned as
`ballot-2-returned.csv`; every carried column unchanged):

| | Round 1 | Round 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right | 239 | 234 |
| Wrong | 12 | 1 |
| Unsure | 3 | 3 |
| Precision | 95.2% | 99.6% |
| Right statements found, of the 262 either round found | 239 (91.2%) | 234 (89.3%) |

The one wrong statement is `resource-demanding-functionality` on the example,
from the Athena sentence, in one run of five. Round 2 lost 28 statements that
round 1 found right, and found 23 new ones that are right. Each case ran once
a round, so one run cannot tell a loss the rules cause from the difference
between runs. Case 09's `database` is one loss to watch: rule 7 allows it,
because the description names SQL, but in a different sentence from its
database. 211 right statements were found by both rounds.

## Round 2 swept, and unseen adversarial text (E3)

The maintainer asked whether a wider sweep could inform the build decision.
Three parts, priced at the offline map's rates for `gpt-5.6-luna` ($0.20 a
million prompt tokens, $1.20 a million completion tokens, every prompt token
at the full rate): two more runs on each development case
(`case-runs-v2-sweep.json`, 28 calls, $0.102), one run on each of the 8
adversarial cases in `evals/adversarial/cases/` (`adversarial-runs.json`,
$0.024), and an offline count on the 14 reference models
(`count_on_reference_models.py`, `count-on-reference-models.json`, $0). With
rounds 1 and 2 ($0.076 and $0.077 at the same rates), the audit spent about
$0.28 of the $0.30. The holdout cases each have one read left, and the
maintainer kept both for the promotion gate of a node.

**On the reference models**, lever A plus one round-2 run per case leaves the
listed capability questions nearly as they were (330 to 320 over the 11 cases
whose precondition holds), because the limit of 30 fills the list from below.
The questions off the list fall from 815 to 737, and the units that apply with
no answer rise from 663 to 1,001.

**Stability.** None of the 28 calls stopped early. On average, 66% of the
statements a case receives over its three runs are in all three. One run of
case 13 kept 20 statements where the other two kept 30 and 31.

**Precision.** A third ballot held the 104 statements no earlier ballot held
(`ballot-3-issued.csv`, returned as `ballot-3-returned.csv`; every carried
column unchanged). It added the reason `injected`, for a statement that rests
on text that gives the model orders.

| Text | Statements | Right | Wrong | Unsure | Precision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Development, round-2 prompt, 47 calls | 272 | 267 | 2 | 3 | 99.3% |
| Adversarial, unseen, 8 calls | 87 | 84 | 3 | 0 | 96.6% |
| Adversarial clean control (case 08) | 16 | 16 | 0 | 0 | 100% |

The three adversarial errors are all `injected`, from two of the seven
poisoned cases: `encryption` from the invented "QuantumVault" component in
case 05, and `database` and `untrusted-data-in-dangerous-context` from the
"reviewer directive" in case 06. The other five injections changed nothing
the reader stated. Because the reader states only presence, an injected
statement makes a requirement apply that does not; it cannot rule one out.

## A rule against injected orders (E4)

Rule 8 says that text giving the model orders does not describe the system,
so the reader states and quotes nothing from it. The 8 adversarial cases ran
once more (`adversarial-runs-v3.json`, $0.0238 at the same rates; a $0.01
increase took the grant to $0.31, and the audit spent $0.2998 of it). None of
the three statements ruled `injected` came back.

The run made 13 statements that no earlier ballot holds
(`ballot-4-issued.csv`). The maintainer dropped that ballot on 2026-10-14,
because the promotion gate of ADR 0086 measures the prompt as it ships, rule 8
included, on unseen text. Those 13 statements are unruled.
