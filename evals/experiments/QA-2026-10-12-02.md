# QA-2026-10-12-02: what the adaptive list settles at matched choices

For [#1632](https://github.com/mstarks01/work-agent/issues/1632), part 3: the
evidence for ADR 0078. Every run is offline (`ANALYSIS_OFFLINE=1`), reads
archived reports, and spends $0. No model runs.

## The instrument

`run.py early-answerers` answers the early list from the top, one question a
save, with four answerers (every capability "yes", "no", "I don't know", or
"yes" and "no" by turns over the capability's name), under two orders: the
shipped list, and the list with `answer_round.capability_effects` stubbed to
answer nothing, which is the band-and-units order of ADR 0055 with the
depth-and-name tiebreak. Each walk is read at 5, 10 and 20 choices. A STRIDE
report's conditional finding is complete when every open fact it waits on is
answered; an ASVS report's unit is decided when the framework's own
applicability rule, over the model with the answers written in, no longer says
`unknown`. Both are counts of dependency sets, never of findings an answer
changes.

The archive: 218 reports of 11 cases that hold reports of both frameworks (186
STRIDE, 32 ASVS; 2,796 conditional findings, 5,534 ASVS units). Each report is
read alone and with both frameworks selected.

## The order #1632 asked for: refuted (E1)

The issue ranked a question by the most other questions an answer removes.
`early-answerers.json` reads that order as `effects` and the band-first order
as `no-effects`, at `9fad621e`, the code of #1633.

ASVS alone, 32 reports, units decided (and questions removed), paired by report:

| Answerer | Choices | Potential first | Band first | Reports where potential first decides more / fewer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no | 5 | 2,744 (972) | 3,158 (840) | 0 / 18 |
| no | 10 | 3,752 (1,062) | 3,680 (1,008) | |
| no | 20 | 4,022 (1,062) | 3,914 (1,008) | 18 / 0 |
| alternate | 5 | 2,228 (642) | 2,768 (648) | 0 / 30 |
| alternate | 20 | 3,794 (792) | 3,542 (648) | 18 / 0 |
| yes | 5 | 1,094 (0) | 1,748 (30) | 0 / 30 |
| yes | 20 | 2,660 (48) | 2,876 (30) | 0 / 18 |
| unknown | any | 800 (0) | 800 (0) | |

The potential-first order removes more questions under "no" (+16% at 5
choices) and decides fewer units at 5 choices for every answerer, by 13% to
37%, on 18 to 30 of the 32 reports. It decides more units only past ten
choices, and only for an owner who answers "no" or by turns; for an owner who
answers "yes" it decides fewer at every budget. A "no" to a broad parent
removes many questions and decides few units; the band-first order asks the
capability that decides the most. STRIDE alone is identical under both orders
at every budget: no rule removes a field question. The maintainer chose the
band-first order on 2026-10-12, with the effects as a tiebreak and published
on each row.

## The turns did not carry over the saves: defect reproduced and fixed (E2)

With both frameworks selected, the first reading showed STRIDE completing 45,
96 and 830 findings at 5, 10 and 20 choices, against 946, 1,488 and 1,939
alone. The turn order started from zero charge on every build, so an owner
who answered the first question and saved met the ASVS question first every
time. `answer_round.recommended` now starts the turns from what the saved
answers charged each framework. `early-answerers-all-after.json` reads the
same walks at the fix:

| STRIDE with both selected, 186 reports | 5 choices | 10 | 20 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Completed before the fix (critical or high) | 45 (36) | 96 (61) | 830 (419) |
| Completed after the fix | 929 (538) | 1,342 (728) | 1,958 (1,015) |
| Alone | 946 (521) | 1,488 (772) | 1,939 (1,021) |

ASVS with both selected decides fewer units at each budget after the fix
(all "no": 3,158 → 1,898 at 5 choices), because the choices are shared, which
is the trade the maintainer accepted in `QA-2026-10-09-02-E2`. The figures
are the same under both orders at 5 and 10 choices.

## The build time: measured, then under the target (E3)

The issue proposed a p95 under 500 ms for a save and its recalculation.
Measured on an idle machine, STRIDE and ASVS level 3, 20 builds each:

| Model | Before | After, nothing kept | After, kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shared test model, 7 elements, no answers | p95 574 ms | p95 169 ms | 11 ms |
| shared test model, thirty "yes" answers | p95 548 ms | p95 305 ms | 12 ms |
| largest corpus model (14), 22 elements, no answers | p95 777 ms | p95 180 ms | 14 ms |
| largest corpus model, thirty "yes" answers | p95 760 ms | p95 332 ms | 14 ms |

Three changes: `fact_answers.key_ref` is cached (a build called it thousands
of times); `open_capability_needs` reads a unit's band once; and
`early_questions` and `capability_effects` keep their results by a digest of
what they read. The page's input feedback (p95 under 100 ms) is not measured
here: the node stand-in has no timer worth reading, and no browser profile
was run.

## The effects as a tiebreak: supported (E4)

The shipped order, band and units first with the effects as the tiebreak,
read alone against the depth-and-name tiebreak
(`early-answerers-alone-tiebreak.json`). ASVS units decided are identical at
5 and 20 choices for every answerer; at 10 choices the "yes" owner decides
2,384 against 2,402, one unit fewer on 18 of 32 reports (0.7%). Under "no"
at 5 choices the tiebreak removes 936 questions against 840 for the same
units. STRIDE alone is identical. On the shared test model at ASVS level 2,
thirty "yes" answers leave 18 level 1 requirements unknown under this
tiebreak against 7 under the other: one small model with many ties at thirty
answers, which the archive does not bear out.

## What this does not say

Archive coverage is structural: a complete dependency set is not a grounded
live finding. No live run was made. The holdout cases were not read.
