## Answers

**What is hurting report quality?** The lane picks one place out of several and
writes there. The place it picks is usually a real threat — the maintainer
accepted 16 of the 21 displaced drafts — but it is not the place the corpus
requires. Two thirds of every missed STRIDE must-find sits in this class.

**Which phase is responsible, how confidently, and what alternatives remain?**
Analysis, with high confidence for 20 of the 27 rows. A `place` row means a
candidate rule led the lane to the reference's own elements, so preparation
cannot own it: the material arrived. Preparation owns at most the 7 `unled`
rows. Criticism is ruled out again — the critic kills 17 drafts of 195 and
costs 3 must-finds for good.

**What is the proposed fix?** One instruction in the tampering lane skill, and
nothing else. Tampering is the only lane that carries both a large loss and a
lead the lane does not use. Every other lane fails one half of that test.

**What was changed, and what was measured?** No code and no prompt changed. The
`6bff717` Baseline was scored twice, against two corpora, with one scorer. The
loss rows were split by cause and by displaced draft. All 21 displaced drafts
were joined to the vote ledger. The candidate rules were run over all 13 blessed
models to count the places each lane is led to.

**What did not work?** Two refuted rows. `-E1` rules out the fear that the
2026-09-22 sitting invalidated the published figures; it moved them by under 2
points. `-E4` rules out a blanket instruction to draft at every led place: it
grows output from 182 places to 310, and 98 of the 128 new places land in
information-disclosure and repudiation, which between them lose 5 of the 43
must-finds.

**What remains untested or blocked?** Whether the tampering instruction moves
anything, which needs a paid run. Reviewed precision under a higher volume,
which no instrument predicts offline. Report prose. Anything about ASVS, which
has no merged Baseline.

**What was spent, and what remains?** $0 spent. The budget was $0.

**What should the next audit do first?** Ask the user for one case, in analysis
mode, on the tampering lane's worst case. Case 07 loses 10 `place` rows, more
than any other case. The two probe runs of `QA-2026-09-20-01` cost $0.67 and
$0.58, so budget about $0.65. Read the tampering drafts against the led places
before anybody proposes a corpus sweep.

## Framework parity

The priority 2 fix touches one STRIDE lane skill. **ASVS needs no equivalent,
and neither does any package whose claims carry a catalog identifier.** A lane
driven by a closed requirement set has no choice about how many claims to file:
every requirement in the chapter is asked, and the count is the catalog's. The
instruction applies where a lane chooses how many places to write about, which
is a property of an open claim set rather than of the name STRIDE.

### Ticket: a loss row does not say whether a person accepted the draft beside it

**The blocked question.** A `place` row charges the lane with a miss. It cannot
say whether the lane went silent or wrote an accepted finding at the adjacent
place. Those are two different defects and two different fixes.

**Evidence.** `run.py score` writes `displaced_draft_id` and stops there. To
learn that 16 of the 21 displaced drafts carry an `up` vote, this audit joined
the loss rows to `evals/review/votes/mstarks01.jsonl` by case, endpoint-resolved
elements and verb, by hand.

**The missing capability.** The loss row holds no verdict. `run.py review` reads
the ledger but answers a different question — what a reviewer has waiting. The
vote ledger holds no loss cause. Nothing joins the two.

**Where the change belongs.** The instrumentation. `losses.py` already reads the
ledger's neighbours and already names the displaced draft. Adding the verdict in
the scorer would change what every past `place` figure meant.

**The smallest change.** One field on `Loss`: `displaced_verdict`, the vote on
the displaced draft where one exists and `None` otherwise, read through the same
`endpoint_form` resolution the identity rule uses. Print it in the pooled line
beside the existing displaced count.

**Acceptance.** A fixture whose displaced draft carries a known vote, and a run
of `score` over the `6bff717` Baseline that reads 16 `up` of 21. The field reads
`None` on a tree with no votes, so it runs on a half-built ledger.

**Cost.** Small. No inference cost. No `needs-sweep`.

**Risk and rollback.** It adds a field and changes no number. Remove the field
to undo it.

**Resume.** `QA-2026-09-22-02`, experiment `-E3`.

`evals.harness.triggers._hit` matches on **lane plus element overlap**, and its

reference names makes the row `place`. It does not mean the lane was offered
that place.

So **`-E2`'s claim that analysis owns 20 of the 27 rows is wrong**, and `-E6`
supersedes it. Only 7 of the 20 `place` must-finds have a candidate at the
reference's place as a set:

| lane | at the exact place | `place` must-finds |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| tampering | 3 | 8 |
| spoofing | 2 | 4 |
| elevation-of-privilege | 1 | 2 |
| denial-of-service | 0 | 5 |
| repudiation | 0 | 1 |

Analysis owns at most 7. **Preparation owns the other 13**: nothing proposed
that place to the lane at all.

## The tampering instruction is withdrawn, and the run with it

`-E7` prices it on the rows it can reach. The ceiling is **3 must-finds of 103,
which is 2.9 points against a band of about 3.3**. It sits inside the band, and

No single case can see it either. The 8 tampering `place` must-finds spread over

# The candidate-rule price, 2026-09-23

The maintainer asked for the replacement proposal to be priced offline. It is

clear the band by 1.4 times.** No paid call was made.

## How led the lane really was

`-E6` was too strict and `-E8` supersedes it. A candidate wider than the
reference is still a lead: `endpoint_subset` is the rule the scorer already
matches drafts with, and its docstring gives the reason — two sides written at
different grain are one claim. Read at three grains, the 20 `place` must-finds
sit:

| grain | rows | means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| exact | 6 | a candidate names the reference's place |
| subset | 6 | a candidate's place contains it, at a coarser grain |
| overlap only | 8 | no candidate at or around the place |

So **analysis owns 12 and preparation owns 8**, not the 7 and 13 stated above.
`-E6` also miscounted the exact tier as 7 where the rows sum to 6.

## What a lead is worth

`-E9`, over all 103 must-find references:

| | matched | total | rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| led | 50 | 75 | 0.667 |
| not led | 10 | 28 | 0.357 |

A lead is worth **+0.31** in the rate at which a must-find is found.

**This is a correlation and an upper bound.** The rules fire on structurally
obvious places, so the led group is the easier group, and a rule reaching a
harder reference need not convert at 0.667. `QA-2026-09-20-01-E16` measured that
directly: it added four leads, three named a flow the claim was not about, and
it bought about one must-find.

## The two families, and why they must land together

18 missed must-finds have no candidate at or containing their place. Three sit
outside a lead's reach — two lost to `verb` and one to the critic, where the
lane already wrote at the place — leaving 15.

| family | shape | reachable rows | priced at 0.31 | against a band of 3.37 |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| store | an actor and the store it touches | 9 | 2.8 must-finds | **inside** |
| process | an actor and a process | 6 | 1.9 must-finds | **inside** |
| both | | 15 | **4.7 must-finds** | clears, 1.4× |

The store family is `process+store` 6, `entity+store` 3, and one
`entity+entity+store`. The process family is `entity+process` 5,
`process+process` 2, and one `entity+entity+process`; five of its rows are
elevation-of-privilege, the lane `-E16` already probed.

## What to do next, and what not to

**Build both families and re-read this measurement before anybody pays.** The
lead rate, the conversion crossing and the 18-row list all recompute for free
from the commands in Reproduction above.

Then run `-E16`'s check, which is the one that matters: for each newly led
reference, read the flow or pair the rule fired on against the mechanism the
claim describes. `-E16` failed exactly there. A family whose leads name the
right mechanism keeps the 0.31; one whose leads merely share an element does
not, and its real ceiling collapses the way `-E16`'s did.

**Spend nothing until that check passes on most of the 15.** Even then the
margin is 1.4 times the band, which is one sweep's worth of evidence and no
more.

## The store family was already declined, with a reason

## What the evidence actually points at

Both refutations name the same missing thing, and the repository already named
it first. The references rest on **authority** — who may write this store, and
whether the app separates these two roles — and the System Model carries no
field for it. `_unverified_write_to_store`'s docstring calls that the next
structured fact, in the order `#671` finding 11 asks for: **facts first, then
the rules that read them.**

`Assertion` already carries a free `predicate`, and a rule already takes the
catalog, so the seam exists. The work is an authority predicate and a rule that
reads it — not another predicate over flow attributes.

**That is a design question, not a measurement, and this audit stops here.**

# The authority predicate, 2026-09-23

The maintainer asked for the authority predicate to be pursued. **It already

14 of them as ground truth.** Nothing needs adding to the registry. No paid call

## No predicate is missing

`authorization-grant` — *what this principal may do, to which resource* — has
been in the registry since it was written. Over the 13-case archive
`20260917T-arms-luna-pro`, distinct rows:

| predicate | value kind | rows |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| `authorization-grant` | text | **95** |
| `tenant-ownership` | reference | 44 |
| `administrative-authority` | reference | 8 |
| `represented-by` | reference | 0 |

The rule-readable alternatives are barely reached. And the maintainer signed
**14 `authorization-grant` rows and zero `administrative-authority` rows**, so
steering the model at the reference-valued predicate would move it away from the
ground truth, not toward it. `-E13`.

## The resource is prose because it has to be

Typing the `resource` qualifier as an element reference would make the fact
rule-readable. It would also refuse the rows that matter. Of the 14 signed
resources, **nine name a component** — the order service, the orders database,
the receipt archive, the claims warehouse, the model registry bucket, the build
runner, the identity broker, the store admin console, the web API.

**Five name something the graph has no node for**: the weekly rota for their
store, the shifts they have been given, the payroll export, and twice the crew
movements and depot work assignments.

Two of those five are case 11's pair — *store managers build the weekly rota*
and *colleagues look at the shifts they have been given*. That pair **is** the
role separation case 11's unled elevation must-find rests on. A reference type
refuses exactly the row the lane needs. `-E14`.

## So the fact cannot reach a rule, only the lane

A candidate rule reads elements. A grant's object is often not an element. That
is not a defect to repair; it is what the fact is.

The reader that *can* take it already exists: the Evidence Catalog offers
settled unprojected rows as `assertion:<id>` refs, and `ANALYSIS_ASSERTIONS`
runs the node in production. **It is off by default.**

What the signed facts reach, over the 15 unled missed must-finds:

| | rows |
| --- | ---: |
| a signed **authority** fact speaks about the place | **7** |
| some signed fact speaks about the place | 8 |
| nothing at the place | 0 |

## Why this stops here

**No offline instrument can measure the consumer.** `authorization-grant` has no
`projects_into`, so the projection instrument is blind to it — the property that
already blocks Phase 6. Pricing the 7 rows at `-E9`'s 0.31 borrows a constant
measured for *candidate leads*, and an evidence row reaching a lane is a
different mechanism with no measured conversion at all. It would give about 2.2
must-finds against a band of 3.37, on a number this audit is not entitled to.

The question — does turning `ANALYSIS_ASSERTIONS` on change what the lane
writes — needs a paid A/B on one case and has no offline proxy. That is the
extension ticket below, and it is where this audit ends.

### Ticket: no instrument reads whether an assertion row changed a lane's draft

**The blocked question.** `ANALYSIS_ASSERTIONS` puts settled assertion rows in
front of the lane as evidence refs. Nothing says whether a lane that was offered
an authority row writes a claim it otherwise would not.

**Evidence.** `run.py replay` re-scores archived assertion emissions against the
projection, and `authorization-grant` projects into no graph field, so it scores
nothing. `run.py bind` names the bindings a graph refuses, which is a different
question. The corpus carries 14 signed `authorization-grant` rows and no
instrument reads them against a draft.

**The missing capability.** A crossing of assertion rows against drafts: for one
run, which claims cite an `assertion:<id>` ref, and which of those match a
reference the same run's control arm missed.

**Where the change belongs.** The instrumentation, beside `losses.py`. Putting
it in the scorer would change what every past figure meant.

**The smallest change.** Read the evidence refs each claim carries, already in
the report, and cross them with the loss causes. One column on the pooled block:
how many losses the arm recovered carried an assertion ref.

**Acceptance.** It reads zero on every archived sweep, because none ran with the
flag, and non-zero on a run that does.

**Cost.** Small offline. The A/B it enables is two runs of one case, about
$1.30, and needs the maintainer's permission. Apply `needs-sweep`.

**Risk and rollback.** It adds a column and changes no number.

**Resume.** `QA-2026-09-22-02`, experiments `-E13` and `-E14`.
