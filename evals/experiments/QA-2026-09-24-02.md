## Answers

**What is hurting report quality?** `place` is the largest STRIDE loss class
on every Baseline. In the three runs that captured lane material, a `place`
loss is a lane that **was** shown the reference's place and drafted nothing
there. The lead arrived.

**Which phase is responsible?** Analysis, for these rows. Preparation delivered
the lead: an exact candidate, the elements in the evidence catalog, and the
lane's scope line. `QA-2026-09-22-02-E6` showed that `place` does not imply this
in general; the captured material shows it holds for every must-find row here.

**What is the proposed fix?** None yet. `QA-2026-09-22-02-E4` refuted a blanket
"draft at every led place": the lanes drafted at about half their led places on
6bff717 too (information disclosure 112 led, 58 drafted, 56 listed), so drafting
everywhere doubles output against a reference that lists about as many. A fix
has to be selective, and nothing here says yet which leads to take.

**What did the lanes do instead?** On case 07 the spoofing lane drafted one
claim from five leads, about the shared build token, where the reference lists
three per-flow impersonations. The lane wrote one claim per mechanism rather
than one per place. Two cases cannot say whether that is the pattern.

**What did not work?** Nothing was tried. E2 is inconclusive by sample size.

**What remains untested or blocked?** E3: whether the pass-by is systematic or
run-to-run variance. `run.py lane-replay` answers it for one call per repeat.

**What was spent?** $0.

**What should the next audit do first?** Read E1 and E2 again over the lane
material of the #1177 sweep, which captures 36 runs at no extra cost.

attacker acts. The reference cites registry to store server, where the harm
lands. This is a disagreement about which place a planted-image finding
belongs at, not an omission, and it is the owner's to rule on.

**Next.** The case-01 row needs nothing: the lane finds it one time in three.
The two case-07 rows need the owner's rulings before any prompt change: which

## The fan-in one-hop bound, priced offline (E7, $0)

The fan-in removes a cited element more than one hop from the places a claim's
grounds name (#441). The `fan-in` loss cause (#1171) found it cutting the
reference's element in 18 misses across four Baselines. Because the rule is
deterministic, its price is exact: fan-in re-run over the archived lane
proposals of 52 case-runs, the kept drafts carrying their archived critic
verdict, and re-scored.

| | V0 one hop (shipped) | V1 two hops | V2 no bound |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Must-finds matched | 201 | **206** | 207 |
| All matches | 381 | 394 | 395 |
| Unlisted claims | 86 | 82 | 82 |
| Mean element_jaccard over matches | 0.420 | 0.413 | 0.413 |
| Mean elements per claim | 2.71 | 2.81 | 2.82 |

**Read for meaning, V1's 15 changed matches:** all 5 must-find gains are the
reference's finding; of the 14 gains in total, 12 are correct and 2 are false
(case 01 ref 9, case 13 ref 3); the one match lost (case 05 ref 13) was itself
doubtful. Seven of the twelve are repudiation claims: the store that keeps the
record sits two hops from the flow the grounds cite.

**Caveats.** V0 reproduced the archive in 45 of 52 case-runs; the other 7 hold
older flow-ID spellings, unrelated to the bound. A kept draft keeps its
archived critic verdict, and a critic shown the wider draft could rule
differently. The gain, about 1.2 points of must-find coverage, is below what a
paid sweep can resolve, but it does not need one: the replay is deterministic.

**Decision owed.** Widening the bound reverses part of #441, whose purpose was
element precision, so adopting V1 is the maintainer's decision.

## Critic stability (E10, #1199), approved at about $0.90

Three more `critic-replay` calls on each E9 case, so each of the 85 drafts has
five verdicts: the archived one, E9's and three new. **Spent: $0.6087
reported.** The second and third rounds cost about half the first. The logs
are in `evals/runs/20260924T-critic-stability-1199/`.

| | agreement |
| --- | ---: |
| One call against another, over all sample pairs | 0.846 |
| Majority of three, plug-in | 0.903 |
| Majority of three, jackknife bias-corrected | **0.891** |
| Majority of three, Dirichlet(1/2) posterior | 0.812 |

**The majority of three misses the declared 0.90.** With five samples a draft,
the plug-in figure is biased high, so the bias-corrected 0.891 decides. The
margin is about one draft in a hundred. A three-way split keeps needs-info.

**E9's 58 of 85 was a low draw.** One call agrees with another on 0.846 of
drafts, not on the 0.68 that E9 alone showed.

**The instability is one boundary.** 26 of 85 drafts took more than one
verdict. Of 131 disagreeing pairs, 117 are needs-info against rejected, 9 are
confirmed against needs-info and 5 are confirmed against rejected. About a
dozen drafts split two against three or three against two, and a majority of
three cannot make a near-even draft repeat. The critic almost never changes
whether a draft is confirmed; it changes whether an unconfirmed draft is kept
as needs-info or rejected. So that choice is the one to make a rule in code.

## Where the critic's verdict moves (E11, #1202, $0)

An offline read of the 26 E10 drafts that took more than one verdict, against
Baseline `6bff717`. The replays kept only their verdicts, so each reason below
is the archived call's.

**The unstable drafts depend on a fact that has no attribute in the model.**
Each of the 11 archived rejections says so:

| The fact the draft needs | Rejections |
| --- | ---: |
| integrity, inferred from an unknown `encryption_at_rest` | 3 |
| authorization, inferred from an unknown `authentication` | 2 |
| an audit record (two inferred from a graph-shape absence) | 3 |
| merge or release authority | 2 |
| request capacity | 1 |

The needs-info verdicts on the same kind of draft ask the same question as a
`subject` entry. Unstable needs-info verdicts carry a `subject` entry in 8 of
14 drafts. Stable ones carry one in 2 of 46.

**`critic.md` gives this case two answers.** Step 1 rejects a draft for
`reasoning` when it infers a fact the model does not state. The verdict
section lets needs-info name a fact with no place in the model as a `subject`.
A draft that needs an unstated, unmodelled fact meets both rules, and the
critic picks one per call.

**The stable verdicts follow the grounds.** All 46 stable needs-info drafts
cite an `unknown-attribute` ground. None of the 11 stable confirmed drafts
cites one.

**What the class is worth.** 18 of the 26 unstable drafts match a reference
claim in the Baseline's scoring, and 7 of those are must-finds. A rule that
ruled the class needs-info every time gains about 2.4 must-finds and 7.8
matches in expectation over the six cases. It also keeps the 8 unstable drafts
that match nothing.

## What each critic job adds (E13, $0)

The four Baselines re-scored with today's scorer: 52 case-runs and 867 kept
claims. A claim counts as real where it matches a reference claim or a person
voted it into the pool. Only 12 kept claims carry a vote that says not real, so
the grading rests mostly on reference matches.

| Job | What it did | Against the reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rejections | 30 kills | 17 removed a matched draft (8 must-finds); none removed a draft voted not real; 1 lane kill was a real lane error |
| Severity change | 45 changes on 438 matched claims | 34 moved further from the reference level, 11 closer, in every Baseline; exact agreement 199 → 184 |
| Confidence | 714 of 867 claims rated low | real share 0.65 high, 0.59 medium, 0.52 low |
| Confirmed or needs-info | 149 confirmed, 718 needs-info | real share 0.62 and 0.53 |
| Mitigation soundness | 33 flagged unsound in 6bff717 | no reference to grade it |

**The reference claims are a person's, not an agent's.** The maintainer read
all 13 cases in the Case Sittings of 2026-09-21 to 2026-09-24 and marked every
reference claim: 506 agree, 131 unsure, 36 reject and 24 duplicate, and each
reject, duplicate and unsure mark was acted on. So a rejection that removed a
matched draft removed a finding a person affirmed.

**The severity change makes the report worse against the reference.** Most
changes lower the rating. The sittings showed each claim's text and elements
but not its severity, and no sitting note mentions one, so the reference
levels are still agent-authored. This result is a strong signal and not
proof.

**Confidence carries a weak signal.** It orders the match rate, but by 13
points from high to low, and it rates 82% of claims low.

**What the critic does that the reference cannot grade:** it writes the
questions a needs-info claim asks, it sets aside unknowns a claim does not use,
and it judges the mitigations. Grading those needs a person.
