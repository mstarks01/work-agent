## E1: almost no reference claim rests on a fact no predicate can hold

All 331 reference claims of the 13 tuned cases were read against each case's
source. For each claim, the reading names the fact the claim rests on and what
holds it.

| Class | All claims | Must-finds |
|---|---:|---:|
| `silent`: the source never states the fact | 222 | 88 |
| `predicate`: a registered predicate holds it | 52 | 36 |
| `field`: a System Model attribute holds it | 39 | 23 |
| `structure`: the topology alone | 17 | 6 |
| `none`: stated, and nothing holds it | 1 | 0 |

Two thirds of the claims rest on silence, and a predicate cannot help a claim
the source gives no fact for. Every stated fact but one already has a holder.

**The one fact.** In case 08, group membership and leavers reach the directory
only through a nightly pull. Claim 08 stride 16 rests on it, and claims 08
stride 19 and 08 asvs 2 rest on it in part. The value is a delay in words, so a
predicate for it would reach a lane as evidence and would have no rule.

**Three signed rows may understate their source.** A sitting decides each:

- Case 04: "we have never expired one", while `credential-expiry` and
  `credential-rotation` on the API key read `unknown`.
- Case 04: "Redis has no password on it", while `authentication-mechanism` on
  the feature-store flow reads `unknown`.
- Case 10: the admin reaches the database "from wherever they happen to be —
  there is no jump host", and no row states the database's exposure.

**What this does not measure.** It reads only facts that some reference claim
rests on. A stated fact that no claim uses is not counted. And a predicate that
exists may still go unwritten by the `assert` node, which required-fact recall
measures.

## E2: almost every fact about a principal reaches no element

Every archived assertion emission was re-resolved with today's code: 90
artifacts and 204 case-runs, with the holdout cases and head-only sweeps left
out. Each settled row about a principal or a credential was asked, through the
STRIDE rules' own helpers, whether it reaches a graph element.

| Subject | Rows | Reach an element | Stranded |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| principal | 444 | 1 | 443 |
| credential | 161 | 116 | 45 |

A principal reaches an element only through a stated `represented-by` row, and
the archive holds one. The stranded principal rows are `authorization-grant`
308, `mfa-requirement` 64, `credential-presented` 60 and
`administrative-authority` 11. A credential reaches a flow through
`credential-presented` on that flow, which the node writes often.

The Evidence Catalog still offers every stranded row to the lanes. What the row
loses is every reader that needs a place: the candidate rules, and the scope
checks that `ground_places` serves. So `spoofing-second-factor-stated-absent`
fires on none of the 64 MFA rows.

Of the 34 distinct principals, 16 share a word with exactly one element, and on
reading about 14 of those are the right element. Two would be wrong ("anything
that can reach the service" would land on the order service, and "corporate
laptop" on the corporate web host). That is why a match by name cannot settle
the link.

## E3: a link question per principal places most of those facts

The design: for each principal, the report asks the submitter which element
it is, from the list of elements plus "none of these". The answer is a stated
`represented-by` row, which the #1225 decision of 2026-09-25 lets settle the
fact. The pricing gives each principal the answer one agent would give; seven
principals answer "none of these".

| Figure | Value |
|---|---:|
| Archived runs with a principal | 167 |
| Link questions per report | 2.2 on average, 5 at most |
| Stranded principal rows placed | 351 of 443 |
| New second-factor leads | 62, all in case 01 |
| New standing-credential leads | 13 |

The links place the rows, and two rules then fire where they could not.
Corpus recall barely moves. The only new leads at a must-find sweep B missed
sit at case 01 ref 0, where the lane already drafts at the right place and
loses the claim on its verb. The value is that 351 stated facts gain a place,
not a figure the corpus can score. The 227 placed authorization grants have no
rule, so they serve only the readers that need a place.

## E4: questions chosen before analysis settle about a third as many findings

Sweep B stage 1, 13 tuned cases, 175 needs-info findings. A finding is settled
when a question set answers every open fact it cites.

| When and how the questions are chosen | Questions | Settled |
|---|---:|---:|
| After the report, any open fact, 6 a case | 78 | 129 |
| After the report, model attributes only | 76 | 69 |
| Before analysis, three most-cited attributes by leads, 6 a case | 78 | 46 |
| Before analysis, the same, no cap | 184 | 88 |
| Before analysis, #1225's first rule | 122 | 62 |

The best early ranking reaches two thirds of what the report-ranked attribute
questions reach at the same count, and about a third of the report's full
ranking. The difference is mostly the 79 free-text open facts the lanes raise,
which no question can ask before the lanes run.

**What this cannot see.** The count reads findings written without answers.
An early answer changes what the lanes write: a stated control removes a lead
and a conditional finding, and a stated absence turns one into a confirmed
finding. That is the main claim for asking early, and only a paid pair on a
job that can pause after extraction can measure it.
