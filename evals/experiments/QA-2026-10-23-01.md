## E7: why the per-lane verb enum lost

The archive gave a wrong first figure: 578 of 1982 October STRIDE threats
rejected for their lane. 552 of these come from one experiment, #1413, which
ran `claude-haiku-4-5` on every tier. On `claude-sonnet-5` medium the rate is
19 of 349, and on the archived Claude lane replays of case 01 it is 0 of 260.

So the test used Haiku 4.5, where the effect is large: six lanes of case 01,
three passes for each arm.

| Arm | Drafts | Misfiled | Lane must-finds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| control (`f4805828`) | 131 | 41 | 17 |
| per-lane enum | 159 | 0 | 18 |

The enum removes every misfiled verb, but not the misfiled threats. The narrow
arm wrote 28 more drafts. 58 of its 141 drafts outside the disclosure lane read
as disclosure threats under a verb that the lane allows, for example "Database
encryption at rest is unknown" as `disable` or `escalate`. Before, code rejected
such a draft for its lane. With the enum, only the critic can reject it. The
lever moves a deterministic rejection into a model's judgement, so it is not
merged. The runs are in `evals/runs/20261023T-lane-verb-enum/`.

## E13: the answer wrapper

On the forced tool, `claude-sonnet-5` writes an extraction or a repair as
`{"answer": {...}}`. The schema refuses the extra key, and the re-ask gets the
same wrapper again. So in E10 and E11, 12 of 15 calls failed, and the shipped
Anthropic base tier failed at extraction in most calls. The native route
refuses the extraction schema before generation ("compiled grammar is too
large").

`answer_from_tool_call` now reads the object under a lone `answer` key when
the sent schema has no such field. The schema check then reads that object.
Each unwrap sets `nodes[].answer_unwrapped`. In E14 and E15, every logged raw
call still had the wrapper, and no call failed because of it.

## E14 and E15: the reruns

R3 meets its gate. Each arm passes 4 of 5 archived failures, and both fail case
01 with `assumption-on-unknown`. The median output tokens fall from 6862 to
1188.

R1 fails clause 1 of its gate. With the rule, `encryption_in_transit` agrees on
every flow in all 5 runs. Without it, the value agrees on 4 of 7 flows in 2 of
5 runs. In those runs the model records `https` as an assumption, and
`with_protocol_defaults` derives nothing from an assumed protocol. With the
rule, the model writes the value anyway. So the rule is not a copy of the code
rule, and it stays.

## The maintainer's rulings

The maintainer accepted each recommendation below.

- **C5 (E9, merged):** code writes the lane rejection of a misfiled draft that
  the critic leaves unruled, and leaves `confidence` empty on that ruling.
- **R2 (E8, merged):** the extraction gate uses the ADR 0018 repair rung. The
  offline measurement came first: the rung repairs 21 of 39 distinct archived
  `unverifiable-excerpt` issues, and `meaning_moved` flags none.
- **R1 (E15, refuted):** delete the `encryption_in_transit` rule in
  `extract.md` rule 5, because `with_protocol_defaults` (ADR 0082) writes the
  value. The extract-only scorer reads the model after `after_gate`.
- **R3 (E14, merged):** repair emits a patch in `elements` scope, and
  the whole model in `whole` scope.
- **R4 (E12, refuted):** code computes reach with the operations rule of
  `analyze.md` step 5 and renders it beside each element.
- **R5, waits for a paid run:** the STRIDE skills state a lane's target types
  as the usual case, not a requirement. 22 of 285 corpus references break the
  rule today, and none of them is a must-find.
