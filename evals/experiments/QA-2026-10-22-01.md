## Answers

**What is hurting report quality?** In the later phases on today's archive,
the analysis verb. The `verb` class is the largest must-find charge, or tied
for it, on 9 of the 10 Sonnet 5 runs. The three `elicit` must-finds of cases 14 and 15 are its only loss with no
spread.

**Which phase, and how sure?** Analysis, sure on Sonnet 5 medium. The lane
writes the reference's threat at the reference's elements and picks another
verb. Preparation led the lane there, and criticism, fan-in and reporting kept
what the lane wrote. One alternative stays open: the recommended Claude strong
tier, Opus 5.5 at high effort, may already pick `elicit`.

**What is the proposed fix?** One sentence in `output.md`. It asks who moves
the data: where the attacker reaches the store or a copy of it, the verb is
`read`; where the system returns the data in answer to what the attacker sends
(an unchecked identifier, a redirect target, a crafted document), the verb is
`elicit`, also through a page that anybody can open. Corpus row 09/13 (a
misdirected fax) needs a check against the wording.

**What did not work?** E2: both scorer equivalences.

**Which count is right in E3?** Neither, fully. The fix in #1896 makes `descendants` read the scorer's map, so the two agree. E8 shows that the scorer's map can refuse a correct end-to-end match, and that question waits for a ruling.

**What remains untested?** The live effect of the rule. ASVS in every later
phase, because no ASVS sweep is on today's corpus. Report prose, remediation
and questions, which no instrument reads.

**What was spent?** $0.26 of a $1.75 grant, on one stage A call (E9). Anthropic's content filter stopped the Opus 5.5 information-disclosure lane on case 15, and QA-2026-10-21-01-E2 records the same refusal on case 14. So stage A cannot run on Opus 5.5, and it stopped after one call. Then $1.25 on six Sonnet 5 medium lane calls with the rule (E10), so $1.51 of $1.75 in all.

**What did the rule do on Sonnet 5?** On case 15, one pass wrote `elicit` for 15/5 and 15/6 and matched both. The five archived control lanes match neither. On case 14, the lane missed 14/4 in 3 of 3 passes, and no draft states the prefix-matched redirect at all. The control lanes state that threat in 4 of 5 runs, but the r1 lane, whose material the replays use, also has no draft at that place. So the replays do not show whether the rule or the material removed the threat. Case 01 kept 01/10 and 01/11 in 2 of 2. The rule is not merged.

**How large is the E8 undercount?** Large (E11). On the 5 archived end-to-end sweeps, the drafts were given the corpus flow's ID in place of each renamed extraction flow, and the scorer then matches 28 must-finds of 37 in place of 18. It loses no match. Of the 7 gained must-finds read, 6 state the reference's threat. The scorer is protected, so a change waits for a ruling on ADR 0061.

1. **Question.** On the deployment model, does the lane file 15/5, 15/6 and
   14/4 as `elicit` with today's text, and does the rule move them? The answer
   decides if the rule ships.
2. **Offline checks.** E1 to E3, above.
3. **Ledger.** No prior row matches this signature.
4. **Why no free check.** A verb choice is fresh model output.
5. **Stage and cases.** The information-disclosure lane only, through
   `lane-replay --append` on the archived case 14 and 15 requests, and case 01
   as the control for its `read` must-find 01/10.
6. **Metric.** The `elicit` rows matched a call, of 3, and the `read`
   must-finds kept. Three passes an arm. Accept the rule if it matches at least
   2 of the 3 rows in at least 2 of 3 passes and loses no `read` must-find.
7. **Spend.** About $0.29 for each Opus 5.5 high lane call (estimated, from
   $1.75 a six-lane pass on 2026-10-20). Stage A, the control arm on cases 14
   and 15: 6 calls, about $1.75. Stage B, the rule arm on the three cases and
   the case 01 control: 12 calls, about $3.50. A retry costs one call.
